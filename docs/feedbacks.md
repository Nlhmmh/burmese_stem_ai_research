### Assignment 2

The title is scoped to "Low-Resource STEM Education" generically, while the entire paper, the gap statement, the Venn centre, and all three research questions are specific to Burmese-speaking learners. The title, therefore, under-specifies the research context that the rest of the document treats as constitutive. The abstract asserts a deliverable the body does not contain: it claims the study "derives design principles," but no design principles are stated anywhere in Sections 1–5. The PIRQOA table produces requirements, objectives, and artefact names, which are not the same construct. In a DSR paper, design principles are formally specified objects (aim, mechanism, boundary conditions; following Gregor & Jones, 2007). Promising them in the abstract and delivering a requirements column is an alignment failure between the abstract and progress. Keywords are serviceable but redundant ("Low Resource Languages" / "Burmese Language" / "Multilingual Learning" occupy overlapping semantic space) and omit the disciplinary anchors an IS reader would search on, such as Design Science Research, Information Systems, or Educational Information Systems.

The Open Access filter discarded 121 of 188 records, representing around 64% of the identified corpus, based on a criterion unrelated to topical relevance and not explicitly stated in the PRISMA diagram. The PRISMA diagram compounds this by recording those 121 under "Records removed for other reasons," a field PRISMA 2020 reserves for administrative removals, not for the analyst's own eligibility decision. The result misrepresents where in the pipeline the selection occurred. Additionally, no date range is stated anywhere, so "currency" is unevidenced despite the corpus being visibly recent. Screening was single-rater with no inter-rater agreement or second screener. There is no backward or forward citation chasing, which, for a fragmented literature, is the technique most likely to recover the integrative work the paper claims does not exist.

The heatmap's colour encoding is inverted relative to its own legend. The text states that darker red indicates stronger coverage. Since the heatmap is the analytical instrument from which the entire fragmentation argument and hence the gap is derived, a reader following the visual encoding reaches the opposite conclusion to the one argued in the text. It invalidates the figure as evidence until it is corrected.
The rubric asks the heatmap to map the strengths and weaknesses of papers against themes. What is coded is topical coverage about how much a paper talks about a sub-theme, not how well. A study can score 3 on Context-Aware Translation with a weak single-language evaluation and no baseline, and the instrument cannot distinguish that from rigorous work. There is no methodological quality appraisal anywhere in Section 2: no discussion of sample sizes, evaluation designs, baselines, or generalisability of any reviewed study. The reading is therefore substantially descriptive.
Four of the seventeen included studies, namely Ataman et al. (2025), Yu et al. (2025), Bo et al. (2025), and Yüksel et al. (2024), appear only in the heatmap image and are never discussed in the narrative of 2.1 through 2.4. Yüksel et al. (2024) present TurkishMMLU, a benchmark dataset paper that directly contradicts the stated exclusion criterion applied at the full-text stage. Either the criterion was applied inconsistently, or it is misstated. Both are traceability problems.

The Venn Diagram was used for illustration and not argument. No study is placed in any region, no pairwise or triple intersection is labelled or populated, and no counts appear. A reader cannot verify from the figure that, say, the Terminology × Scaffolding intersection is thin, because the figure carries no data. It restates the conclusion rather than supporting it with evidence. This is the rubric's "present but underused as a tool for argument" almost verbatim. Note also that four circles cannot render a true Venn: the arrangement shown produces twelve of the fifteen required regions, so the figure is an Euler-style approximation, which should be stated or replaced with four ellipses.
Another issue is the form of the gap claim. "No study in the reviewed sample examined an integrated LLM-based STEM scaffolding system for Burmese-speaking learners" is close to tautological, given the search design. The query included "Burmese" and "Myanmar" as two of fourteen terms in a single facet, searched in a single database with a 64% availability filter, so the absence of Burmese work is substantially an artefact of corpus construction rather than a demonstrated state of the field. The paper needs to argue that the absence matters, which tells that Burmese learners face a documented STEM attainment or access problem, with evidence external to the corpus, not merely that nobody has published on it.

Every system is a research prototype reported in an included study, so the SSR inherits all the biases of the SLR search and adds no independent coverage. The systems landscape that a Burmese learner actually encounters is entirely absent: ChatGPT and Gemini used directly, Google Translate, Khanmigo, Photomath and its solver competitors, Duolingo, and any Burmese-specific language technology or terminology resource. Since the paper's practical problem is defined by what learners can and cannot currently do, omitting deployed tools means the stated system-level gap is unproven against the strongest incumbents. The obvious challenge is "why is this not solved by pasting the term into ChatGPT," and the SSR as constructed cannot answer it.
Methodologically, no selection protocol is given: no search strategy for systems, no inclusion criteria, no access dates, no version numbers, and no a priori comparison dimensions. The five assessment columns were evidently derived after the systems were chosen. All limitations are inferred from what the source papers report rather than from inspection, which the author honestly signals with "does not report" phrasing. This means the limitations column records reporting gaps, not capability gaps.

Scaffolding Theory is invoked through Wood et al. (1976) alone, with no engagement with the Vygotskian ZPD it operationalises, no treatment of fading and contingency, and no reference to the software-scaffolding literature that addresses precisely the problem of relocating scaffolding from a human tutor to a system. Quintana et al. (2004) and Puntambekar and Hübscher's critique of distributed scaffolding are the obvious omissions, and their absence matters because an LLM cannot perform contingent diagnosis the way a tutor can. CTML is cited once and applied in a single sentence about not increasing cognitive demand. Its actual design implications (the modality, redundancy, and coherence principles, all of which are directly relevant to presenting bilingual terminology alongside explanation) are unused. The DSR grounding rests solely on Hevner et al. (2004), without Gregor and Hevner (2013) on knowledge-contribution positioning or Gregor and Jones (2007) on what a design theory must specify, which is why the abstract's promised design principles have nowhere to attach. Within the table, the second problem cell repeats the same sentence. The requirements are single-clause and not yet specific enough to be verifiable. The objectives largely restate the RQs in a different grammatical person. There is no column that traces each row to the SLR theme, heatmap column, or SSR row that generated it. That missing traceability is exactly the "linkage to SLR/SSR could be tighter" descriptor. No evaluation criteria are stated for any artefact.

There is no introduction establishing the problem before the method appears, and no conclusion. The paper ends mid-argument on the PIRQOA commentary. All headings, including 2.1 through 2.6 and 5.1 and 5.2, are set to Heading 1 in the document styles, so the file has no navigable hierarchy and no automatic table of contents. There are no page numbers in any footer. The heatmap is embedded as a raster image but is captioned "Table 2," which mislabels it as a table under APA 7, where it should be a figure. Figure 2 is captioned after the paragraph that discusses it and at the very end of Section 3.1, separating the caption from the first mention.

Four reference-list entries, such as Ataman et al. (2025), Bo et al. (2025), Yu et al. (2025), and Yüksel et al. (2024), are never cited in the running text. They appear only as row labels within a raster image, which means that, under a strict reading, they are uncited works in the list, and a text-based similarity or citation check would flag them as such. Several specific empirical claims attributed to sources lack a locator despite being precise enough to warrant one, notably the Kleidermacher and Zou claim about selective English retention, which is a specific finding that does real load-bearing work in both Section 2.2 and Section 3.
