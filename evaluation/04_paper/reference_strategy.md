## 1. Selection and citation rules

Use Hevner et al. (2004) for IS evaluation and literature-grounded informed
argument, Goodhue and Thompson (1995) for task–technology alignment, and van
de Pol et al. (2010) for the operational scaffolding benchmark. TTF remains
the principal IS theory; Scaffolding Theory is complementary. FEDS supports
evaluation-scope interpretation, not a replacement kernel theory. Sweller is
supplementary and does not retrospectively make CTML the principal theory.

For each substantive argument, write: **cited research warrant → project
mechanism → counterargument/transfer limit → recorded result and qualified
conclusion**. Do not use literature merely to decorate a description. The
literature does not specify this project's exact topology, route permissions,
two-round maximum or High-to-fade policy; those remain design decisions.

Distinguish source access:

- **Inspected relevant original text:** paraphrase only the inspected warrant,
  identify its locator and retain domain/population limits.
- **Original abstract/excerpt:** limit the claim to that abstract/excerpt;
  do not report uninspected methods, effect sizes or whole-study appraisal.
- **Bibliographic metadata only:** use for identity, not a source-specific
  substantive finding. Inspect the actual passage before asserting it.
- **Assignment-mediated summary:** explicitly attribute the comparison to
  Assignment 2, or use an APA secondary citation. Do not imply the original
  paper was read or insert it into the final list as a directly consulted source.

Keep primary-source checking and new supplementary references separate from
the frozen Assignment 2 search. They are **not added to the frozen** 17-study
corpus or attributed retrospectively to GENAI-01/historical analyses.
