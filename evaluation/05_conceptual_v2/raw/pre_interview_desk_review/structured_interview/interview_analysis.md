**Target:** CA1, CA2 and CA3 at baseline CA-V2-20261023

## Method and context

This report records a structured critical question-and-answer assessment. It is analytical critique, not participant testimony or an independent expert interview. The nine topics continue the first iteration: coverage, coherence, theory, completeness, decisions, scenario use, risk, focused improvements and final judgement.

The assessment context consists of the [exact diagram register](../baseline/artefact_register.md), REQ-01–03/RQ1–RQ3, Task–Technology Fit, Scaffolding Theory and the earlier refinement decisions R01–R18. The supplied diagrams are the evidence target. System route tables and earlier presentation images do not supply missing diagram features.

The full questions and responses follow. They are the substantive assessment record, not a claimed transcript of an external conversation. Later literature checks, informed arguments and descriptive cases examine the same claims through different forms of evidence. Familiarity with iteration 1 can direct attention to known concerns and is a source of confirmation bias.

## Q1 — Requirement coverage

**Question:** Do the three v2 artefacts jointly address all three requirements? Identify explicit support and any requirement that depends on additional interpretation.

**Response:** The concept set names terminology identification and context-sensitive language support for REQ-01, conceptual explanation for REQ-02, and structured/adaptive support for REQ-03. The relationship model links the problem, proposed support, intended outcome and learner response. The framework allocates terminology/context/language to Stages 1–3, explanation and learning aids to Stages 4–5, and response/adaptation to Stages 6–7. No requirement lacks a broad conceptual responsibility.

Coverage is less complete at the decision level. Stage 3 says “Retain useful terms”, which is a useful principle but does not state when Burmese explanation, English retention or both should be selected. The research context supplies the target language, but an isolated diagram does not. Requirement coverage is therefore supported at the responsibility level, with qualification for explicit language treatment and adaptive decision principles. This is not evidence that the problems have been solved in use.

## Q2 — Logical coherence and feedback

**Question:** Are the definitions, relationships and process mutually consistent? Trace the feedback arrows and identify any decision or correction gap.

**Response:** The three artefacts have complementary roles rather than competing stage counts. Five concept categories can be realised through seven responsibilities. The core-meaning subtitle and selectable-aid subtitle make Stages 4 and 5 distinguishable without asserting that explanation and scaffolding never overlap.

Stage 7 now has explicit destinations at Stages 3, 4 and 5. A language-related request can return to language selection, a meaning-related request to the core explanation, and another-example request to scaffolding. Each then reaches response collection through the solid process. These are coherent routes.

However, optional help choice and “if needed” do not specify how a reported request is interpreted or prioritised. The Stage 7 subtitle lists actions but does not explicitly assign interpretation before selection. “Clarify ambiguity if needed” in Stage 2 supports an initial ambiguity response. It does not specify how a wrong interpretation discovered after support returns from Stage 7 to Stage 1/2. That exception must not be assumed from the earlier system design. A pause option is named, but its exit and continuation conditions are not drawn.

## Q3 — Consistency with the two theories

**Question:** Which aspects are consistent with Task–Technology Fit and Scaffolding Theory, and which theoretical claims remain unestablished?

**Response:** The capability set is relevant to the stated terminology, language and explanation tasks. That is a task-alignment rationale. A title containing “Task-Aligned” does not establish perceived or measured fit, usage or performance improvement.

The feedback mechanism permits support to respond to an expressed need. Selectable examples, hints and reflection allow changes in assistance. Reduction and pause are now explicit options. This is consistent with some scaffolding design intentions. It remains weaker than demonstrating support calibrated to actual competence: self-report and a help preference do not establish a learner's performance level. Pausing because no further help is requested is not evidence of gradual fading calibrated to learning. Independent performance and transfer of responsibility are not represented or measured. Theory consistency is partial and should be described as an informed design basis.

**Finding links:** V2-F05, V2-F09, V2-F11.

## Q4 — Completeness and redundancy

**Question:** Do the refinements remove duplication or expose a need for another concept, construct or stage?

**Response:** The distinction between core meaning and aids addresses the earlier Stage 4/5 wording problem. Their responsibilities can overlap in one generated explanation while remaining useful for analysis: one asks what meaning must be conveyed; the other asks how assistance is organised.

No examined requirement demands an eighth core stage. Interpreting feedback can be made explicit inside Stage 7. Ambiguity handling can remain within target/context work. A content-quality limitation should be acknowledged and evaluated separately; it does not logically prove that this framework must add a content-verification stage.

Nor does the evaluation prove that exactly five categories, four constructs or seven stages are uniquely necessary. Alternative decompositions could meet the requirements. The framework's lower-right subtitle is crowded, which affects communication rather than conceptual completeness.

**Finding links:** V2-F04, V2-F10, V2-F12, V2-F13.

## Q5 — Decision points and control

**Question:** Does v2 say enough about selecting language support, interpreting optional help choices and selecting/reducing/pausing assistance? What happens if the optional choice is skipped?

**Response:** V2 improves the available input and action vocabulary. Stage 6 distinguishes reported need from an optional help type, and Stage 7 permits selection, reduction or pause. Explicit returns can represent different kinds of request.

The decision principles remain incomplete. “Useful” requires a reference to technical context, recognisability and expressed language need rather than an assumption about nationality or proficiency. Where a help type is present, its content should constrain the route. A request for another example is not a reason to change the target concept. Where the choice is skipped, the broad signal alone cannot identify whether language, core meaning or support form is the difficulty. A clarification or modest default could be defensible, but neither is specified by these images.

A conceptual model need not contain an exact implementation algorithm. It should make clear that reported need is interpreted before a route is chosen, and that unresolved need can lead to further clarification or review instead of indefinite automatic repetition. The exact two-revision implementation cap must remain a separate engineering rule.

**Finding links:** V2-F03, V2-F05, V2-F06, V2-F07, V2-F09.

## Q6 — Scenario applicability

**Question:** Apply the refinements to photosynthesis. Can they represent another example, language help, core clarification and reduced support? What changes when the intended meaning is initially or later ambiguous?

**Response:** A photosynthesis inquiry gives a clear biological target. Core meaning can be separated from a rice-plant example. A constructed request for another example can use the Stage 7 → Stage 5 loop; a language request can use Stage 7 → Stage 3; a core-meaning question can use Stage 7 → Stage 4. These branches expose a stronger process representation than the original single return.

Reduced support is an action option, but no resulting exit state is specified. Initial ambiguity can be considered within Stage 2. A later statement that “cell” meant a battery cell rather than a biological cell needs target/context reconsideration, which the drawn Stage 7 returns do not reach. The scenarios can therefore demonstrate representability of several routes and reveal limits, not successful interactions or learner outcomes.

**Finding links:** V2-F07, V2-F08, V2-F09, V2-F14.

## Q7 — Risks and claim limits

**Question:** Which important risks remain after refinement? Check the relationship model's wording and distinguish a wording fix from an unresolved educational claim.

**Response:** The intention qualifiers and “Proposed outcome” reduce the risk that the model is read as a measured effect. The image's “intended to supports” requires a grammatical correction. The barrier “hinders” arrow remains a general motivating assumption, not a result from this episode.

More substantial risks persist: a confident self-report can be inaccurate; a language strategy can be unsuitable; a fluent core explanation can be wrong; an example can mislead; and support can be internally coherent while explaining the wrong sense. More precise labels do not remove these risks. No current diagram measures fit, validates Burmese terminology, diagnoses misconceptions or demonstrates retention. These are limits of the available evidence, not reasons to invent successful evaluations or add every possible safeguard as a core stage.

**Finding links:** V2-F02, V2-F03, V2-F08, V2-F10, V2-F11.

## Q8 — Improvements within scope

**Question:** What is the smallest useful set of further changes? Preserve the original design and avoid turning a conceptual framework into software architecture.

**Response:** Correct the model grammar. Clarify the language-selection principle in a short caption or subtitle, naming context-sensitive Burmese/English support and useful English terms. Make feedback interpretation explicit inside Stage 7. Add a concise exception note for target/context correction discovered after support. Explain that less reported need may reduce/pause assistance without certifying mastery, and that unresolved need permits clarification/review. Increase the Stage 5 text margin.

These are focused wording and boundary changes. Exact routing rules, provider calls, stored states and numerical limits belong in system artefacts. Quizzes, learner profiling, an eighth evaluation stage and major redesign are not required by this assessment.
