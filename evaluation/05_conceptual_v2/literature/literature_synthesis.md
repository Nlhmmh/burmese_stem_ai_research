The [iteration 1 matrix](../../01_conceptual/literature/literature_matrix.csv) contains **18 finding rows**, L01–L18. All 18 earlier rows were checked for relevance to v2. Repeated concerns were consolidated into 14 current claim appraisals below. These are not 14 new studies. No new systematic search, screening, PRISMA count or heatmap was produced. Selected primary-source pages were consulted to check important warrants. The [source record](source_access_and_references.md) distinguishes those checks from inherited review summaries and failed access.

Support is judged for a **design principle**, not for an educational effect. Stronger primary access does not remove differences in language, age, discipline, task or supervision.

## Important qualifications

The source checks strengthen the rationale for selective term retention and structured multilingual assistance, but also sharpen the transfer boundary. In particular, Kuzu (2026) places productive multilingual activity in a guided instructional context. That is not evidence that self-directed generation can reproduce the same support. Athukorala and De Silva (2025) concerns Sinhala Java assistance, not general Burmese STEM learning.

The reappraisal supports the earlier core-meaning/scaffolding distinction. It does not establish that their content must never overlap. It also supports treating optional help choice as a request rather than an assessment. No source in this review establishes the exact route mapping, the numerical adaptation cap, a universal language strategy or the sufficiency of self-report.

The representation of feedback destinations has improved. The literature does not resolve how to choose a destination when the help choice is absent, how to repair a late context mismatch or when repeated support should be paused/reviewed. Those remain conceptual decisions rather than facts that can be copied from another language system.

C1 has literature-grounded responsibility coverage. C2's exact decisions remain partly specified. C3 is theory-informed, with explicit limits on diagnosis, fading and transfer.
