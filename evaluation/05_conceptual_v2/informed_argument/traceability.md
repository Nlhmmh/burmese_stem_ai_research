# Conceptual informed argument — iteration 2

**Record:** CV2-ARG-01

**Target:** Three v2 conceptual artefacts, baseline CA-V2-20261023

## Method

Ten claims were examined through requirement relevance, a scholarly warrant, the proposed mechanism, a removal test, a counterargument and a qualified conclusion. Removal tests are logical comparisons, not experimental ablations. Literature facts and access limits are consolidated in [the source record](../literature/source_access_and_references.md) and [literature reappraisal](../literature/literature_synthesis.md). Applications of those warrants to these diagrams are design inferences.

## IA01 — The three artefacts make complementary contributions

**Requirement:** REQ-01–03.

**Warrant:** Assignment 3 distinguishes definition, relationships and behaviour. Task–Technology Fit supplies the general relevance question: capabilities should serve the task (Goodhue & Thompson, 1995; inherited substantive warrant, current metadata check only).

**Mechanism and removal test:** CA1 defines five forms of support. CA2 explains why support and learner response matter to the problem. CA3 allocates operational responsibilities. Removing CA1 would leave stage labels without a clear concept set; removing CA2 would weaken the explicit problem/support rationale; removing CA3 would leave no process or feedback destinations.

**Counterargument:** The same knowledge could be documented differently, including in one integrated model. Three diagrams are a useful communication choice, not a theoretically necessary count.

**Judgement:** Supported as complementary representations. V2-F01, V2-F12.

## IA02 — Intervention effects should remain proposed relationships

**Requirement:** REQ-01–02.

**Warrant:** Task alignment motivates a potential contribution to performance; it does not establish this artefact's effect. The study's own requirements distinguish developing support from proving learning.

**Mechanism and removal test:** “Intended to reduce” and “Proposed outcome” keep the intervention rationale visible without reporting effects that were not measured. The right-hand support arrow reads “indented to supports”, so its apparent intended-effect meaning is an interpretation pending wording correction. Removing qualified-effect wording would make the figure easier to misread as a causal result.

**Counterargument:** Qualifying arrows does not make the underlying theory true. Language barriers are not the only influence on understanding, and useful software does not guarantee use or benefit. The model is a simplified rationale rather than a complete causal model. “Leads to” also presumes participation.

**Judgement:** Supported with qualification. Correct “indented to supports” to “intended to support” and describe all arrows as conceptual relationships. “Leads to” also needs a participation qualification. V2-F02, V2-F11, V2-F15.

## IA03 — Terminology and context are distinct, connected responsibilities

**Requirement:** REQ-01–02.

**Warrant:** Domain-specific term identification is the relevant concern in Tran et al.'s (2023) consulted preprint; the existing review uses the later 2026 publication identity separately.

**Mechanism and removal test:** Establishing a target lets later support be evaluated against a concept. Context distinguishes meanings of shared spellings such as “cell”. Removing context can leave a technically correct explanation of an unintended sense. Removing target identification obscures what the support concerns. V2 now recognises ambiguity in Stage 2.

**Counterargument:** Context can influence target identification, so “determines” is best read as information flow rather than a deterministic one-way law. Separate responsibilities need not be separate calls or screens. A stage label does not establish extraction or disambiguation accuracy.

**Judgement:** Supported with qualification. Initial ambiguity is recognised; later correction remains incomplete. “Determines” should be read as information flow or clarified to avoid implying an exclusively one-way dependency. V2-F01, V2-F08, V2-F15.

## IA04 — Language selection needs more than term retention

**Requirement:** REQ-01.

**Warrant:** Selective technical-term retention has a directly inspected rationale in scientific translation, but its target population differs (Kleidermacher & Zou, 2026).

**Mechanism and removal test:** A learner can retain a recognisable English term while receiving Burmese meaning/context. Removing purposeful language selection makes the treatment incidental. V2's retention subtitle improves this responsibility but does not specify the selection of explanation language.

**Counterargument:** Retained English can remain inaccessible; translating every term can also create unfamiliar labels. Neither strategy is universally superior. Familiarity cannot be inferred from being Burmese-speaking. The design needs factors, not an unvalidated universal rule.

**Judgement:** Supported with qualification; R03 remains partly addressed. A concise companion explanation should name technical context, useful/recognisable English terms and expressed language need. V2-F03.

## IA05 — Core meaning and selectable aids should remain distinguishable

**Requirement:** REQ-02–03.

**Warrant:** The existing review distinguishes explanation from structured learning support. Van de Pol et al. (2010) supplies a benchmark for examining the purpose and means of assistance.

**Mechanism and removal test:** Stage 4 specifies what must be explained. Stage 5 selects an example, analogy, hint or reflection to make that meaning accessible. Removing Stage 4 can leave engaging aids without an explicit meaning; removing Stage 5 removes responsibility for choosing assistance beyond the core account.

**Counterargument:** Explanation can itself be a scaffold. Content can overlap, and the same response may fulfil both responsibilities. A longer or more varied response can add burden or confusion. V2 does not prove the aids are suitable.

**Judgement:** Supported as an analytical distinction; no merger is required. V2-F04.

## IA06 — Reported need is useful input without being a competence measure

**Requirement:** REQ-03.

**Warrant:** The retained scaffolding benchmark expects responsiveness. Dunlosky and Rawson (2012) provides a caution about inaccurate self-evaluation; its key-term learning setting is not this interface.

**Mechanism and removal test:** Stage 6 obtains expressed need and an optional help type before assistance changes. Removing this input would make adaptation depend on assumptions or defaults. A help type can distinguish a requested example from a language request.

**Counterargument:** The signal can reflect preference, confidence or fatigue. A skipped choice leaves the cause unresolved; a selected choice is still not diagnosis. V2 appropriately labels the signal, but does not validate its accuracy.

**Judgement:** Supported with qualification. This improves claim clarity and request specificity, not measurement validity. V2-F05.

## IA07 — Adaptation needs interpretation and a relevant destination

**Requirement:** REQ-01–03.

**Warrant:** Task alignment and responsive assistance are the relevant benchmarks. The literature does not prescribe these exact arrows or a unique route algorithm.

**Mechanism and removal test:** Stage 7 can select among language, core meaning and support form. Removing the new Stage 3/4 returns would require an unsuitable example-only response to some requests. Removing interpretation risks selecting any available action without regard to the request.

**Counterargument:** The dashed destinations are explicit, but “if needed” supplies no complete criterion. The action subtitle omits interpretation. Broad need without a help type may justify asking for clarification rather than assuming a specific difficulty. Exact software rules are unnecessary here, but the decision responsibility should be clear.

**Judgement:** Partly supported. Keep the routes and explicitly locate request interpretation within Stage 7. Clarify what to do when need remains unresolved. V2-F06, V2-F07.

## IA08 — Late context correction is an exception that needs an explicit boundary

**Requirement:** REQ-01–02.

**Warrant:** Meaning-sensitive support follows from the requirements and the retained context/terminology evidence. The following failure analysis is a design inference, not an observed error frequency.

**Mechanism and removal test:** If “cell” was interpreted biologically but the learner later specifies a battery, repeating Stages 3–5 preserves the wrong target. The ability to clarify ambiguity during Stage 2 does not itself specify re-entry after support. Removing any correction possibility permits coherent but irrelevant assistance.

**Counterargument:** A new inquiry could be an acceptable correction mechanism. A universal reverse loop is unnecessary. V2 must state its chosen exception—target/context reconsideration or a clearly requested restart—rather than imply that language or core-explanation revision always repairs intent.

**Judgement:** Partly supported. Initial clarification is visible; the late-correction exception remains unspecified. V2-F08.

## IA09 — Reduction and pause do not establish calibrated fading or transfer

**Requirement:** REQ-03.

**Warrant:** The scaffolding benchmark separates responsiveness, fading and transfer (van de Pol et al., 2010). The current evidence supplies a self-report signal rather than demonstrated competence.

**Mechanism and removal test:** “Reduce or pause” makes less assistance a permitted action. Without that option, the loop could imply always adding more. A clear pause/review boundary would also prevent the diagram from implying endless adaptation.

**Counterargument:** One pause is not evidence of gradual withdrawal. A positive report can be wrong, and stopping generation is not the same as independent task performance. No precise support dose is established. A diagram need not add a quiz or learner profile to acknowledge these limits.

**Judgement:** Supported only as an intended action option. Calibrated fading, stopping conditions and transfer remain unestablished. V2-F07, V2-F09, V2-F11.

## IA10 — Retaining scope is defensible, but does not guarantee content reliability

**Requirement:** REQ-01–03.

**Warrant:** The existing review and Ji et al. (2024, consulted preprint version 7) warn against equating fluent generation with correctness. Hevner et al. (2004) supports evaluating usefulness rather than assuming it from construction.

**Mechanism and removal test:** Seven responsibilities cover the stated support cycle. Interpreting need fits within Stage 7. No examined omission requires a new core stage. However, a false explanation can traverse every stage, and an inappropriate aid can be structurally well formed.

**Counterargument:** Content safeguards may be necessary in deployment; their absence is consequential. That does not prove that one more labelled conceptual box would verify content. Independent scientific and bilingual assessment, and later learner evidence, address different risks.

**Judgement:** Retain the scope with explicit reliability limits. No claim of superiority, universal correctness or demonstrated learning follows from this argument. V2-F10, V2-F12, V2-F14.

## Overall interpretation

The rationale for the responsibilities remains defensible. V2 resolves important definitional and feedback-destination ambiguity, but language selection, feedback interpretation, late context repair and support reduction need clearer boundaries. These are specific conceptual clarifications. None of the ten removal tests establishes a unique architecture or an educational effect.
