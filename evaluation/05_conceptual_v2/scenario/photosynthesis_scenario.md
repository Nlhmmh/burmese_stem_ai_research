# Descriptive scenario evaluation — iteration 2

**Record:** CV2-SCN-01

**Target:** CA1–CA3, especially the CA3 feedback branches

**Baseline:** CA-V2-20261023

## Method and limits

Ten constructed cases were reasoned through the supplied diagrams. The first establishes a photosynthesis support situation; Cases S02–S06 are alternative branches from that situation. Cases S07–S10 challenge ambiguity, late correction, self-report and content reliability. They are not one learner's sequential history.

For each case, the condition and expected responsibility are stated before tracing the visible route. The judgement distinguishes an explicit path from a necessary interpretation or missing capability. Example explanations and learner statements below are scenario content, not collected participant responses, observed application outputs or historical quotations from iteration 1.

The scientific basis for the photosynthesis illustration is [OpenStax Biology 2e, Section 8.1](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/8-1-overview-of-photosynthesis) (Clark et al., 2018). The scope is an introductory account. Burmese sentences illustrate language treatment and are accompanied by English meaning; this evaluation does not score their linguistic or instructional quality.

## S01 — Initial photosynthesis support

**Condition:** The inquiry explicitly requests Burmese and useful English terms. The target is photosynthesis in plant biology.

**Expected responsibilities:** Stage 1 establishes the target, Stage 2 fixes the intended domain, and Stage 3 applies the stated language request. Stage 4 conveys the core process; Stage 5 adds a chosen aid.

**Constructed core meaning:**

> Photosynthesis uses light energy to make carbohydrates from carbon dioxide and water. In plants, this process releases oxygen.

**Burmese illustration:**

> Photosynthesis ဆိုတာ အပင်တွေက နေရောင်ခြည်စွမ်းအင်၊ ရေနဲ့ carbon dioxide ကို အသုံးပြုပြီး သကြားဓာတ်ပါတဲ့ အစာကို ပြုလုပ်တဲ့ လုပ်ငန်းစဉ်ပါ။

**Intended English meaning:** Plants use light energy, water and carbon dioxide to make sugar-containing food. “Photosynthesis” and “carbon dioxide” are retained technical labels. Their retention is a scenario choice responding to the request, not a validated Burmese terminology policy.

A selected reflection could ask the learner to identify the inputs in the example. It need not be accompanied by every available hint and analogy. This illustrates CA1's core-meaning/selectable-aid distinction and CA3 Stages 4/5.

**Judgement: Representable.** The responsibilities accommodate the inquiry. Appropriate wording, completeness and actual learning remain separate judgements. CA2's proposed outcome is not observed.

## S02 — Another example: return to scaffolding

**Condition:** Following S01, a constructed response reports continued need and selects “another example”.

**Expected response:** Keep the concept and scientific meaning; change the concrete application.

**Constructed aid:**

> In a rice field, leaves use light energy while the plant takes in water through its roots and carbon dioxide from the air. The sugars made support growth.

**Burmese illustration:**

> စပါးပင်က အမြစ်ကနေ ရေကို စုပ်ယူပြီး လေထဲက carbon dioxide နဲ့ နေရောင်ခြည်စွမ်းအင်ကို အသုံးပြုကာ အရွက်ထဲမှာ သကြားဓာတ်ပါတဲ့ အစာကို ပြုလုပ်တာကြောင့် ကြီးထွားနိုင်ပါတယ်။

**Intended English meaning:** The sentence connects root water uptake, atmospheric carbon dioxide, light energy and food production in a rice plant. It is an aid around the same core meaning.

**Trace:** Stage 6 supplies the request, Stage 7 selects a scaffold change, and the solid outer loop returns to Stage 5. Stage 5 then leads to another response opportunity.

**Assumption:** Interpreting “another example” as an aid request is a reasoned use of the request, not an exact route rule printed in the image.

**Judgement: Representable.** The route destination is explicit. Example suitability and learner benefit are not measured.

## S03 — Language help: return to language selection

**Condition:** The learner statement is “The English term carbon dioxide is unclear; please explain that term in Burmese.”

**Expected response:** Reconsider the language treatment of the term while preserving the intended biology concept.

**Trace:** Stage 6 → Stage 7 → dashed “revisit language” → Stage 3 → Stage 4 → Stage 5 → Stage 6.

**Conceptual result:** The framework can now represent revision of language support rather than forcing an example-only change. A term can remain visible in English while its meaning is explained in Burmese.

**Remaining issue:** “Retain useful terms” alone does not specify which language strategy follows the request. The trace needs the stated request as a selection factor; the companion conceptual explanation should make that principle explicit. It is not evidence that any particular translation is clear.

**Judgement: Representable**, with the above selection assumption retained in interpretation. The broader language-selection gap remains V2-F03.

## S04 — Core clarification: return to meaning

**Condition:** The statement is “Does the plant get its food directly from soil? Please explain the main idea.”

**Expected response:** Revisit the core account before selecting another aid. The question gives explicit content needing clarification; no hidden misconception diagnosis is assumed.

**Constructed clarification:** Soil supplies water and minerals, while the plant makes sugars through photosynthesis. Light supplies energy rather than replacing the material inputs.

**Trace:** Stage 6 → Stage 7 → dashed “revisit core meaning” → Stage 4 → Stage 5 → Stage 6.

**Conceptual result:** The new destination distinguishes revision of an explanatory relationship from changing only the example. The same target remains active.

**Judgement: Representable.** The arrow supports the route. The scenario does not prove that an autonomous system identifies or corrects misconceptions accurately.

## S05 — Less help needed

**Condition:** The response is “I do not need more help now.”

**Expected responsibility:** Allow less support without certifying competence or assuming session completion.

**Trace:** Stage 6 → Stage 7. The Stage 7 subtitle explicitly permits “reduce or pause support”. The only drawn outgoing paths are returns to earlier support responsibilities.

**Additional interpretation required:** A caption could distinguish reducing the amount of assistance, pausing further assistance and choosing to resume/review. The image does not define these conditions.

**Judgement: Partly specified.** An action option is present; calibrated fading, a clear exit and transfer of responsibility are not established. No “mastered” or “completed” outcome is inferred.

## S06 — Help choice skipped

**Condition:** Continued need is reported, but no help type is supplied.

**Expected responsibility:** Interpret what is known and preserve uncertainty about what is not known.

**Trace:** Stage 6 → Stage 7 is available. The signal does not distinguish a language difficulty from a core-meaning question or preference for another aid.

**Alternatives:** Asking a bounded clarification or offering a modest default aid could each be reasonable. These are proposals, not visible rules. Selecting the language branch as if the difficulty were diagnosed would overstate the signal.

**Judgement: Partly specified.** The feedback architecture exists, but the selection principle is underdetermined. No exact software default is borrowed from the system evaluation.

## S07 — Initial ambiguity

**Condition:** The inquiry is only “Explain cell”.

**Expected responsibility:** Recognise that the technical sense is unresolved and seek the intended domain.

**Trace:** Stage 1 identifies the shared term. Stage 2's “Clarify ambiguity if needed” can accommodate a clarification such as “Do you mean a biological cell or a battery cell?” That question is constructed, not an observed output.

**Additional interpretation required:** The image does not show how clarification is collected, how it affects the target or when proceeding to Stage 3 is justified. These can remain internal to terminology/context work; a permanent normal reverse loop is unnecessary.

**Judgement: Partly specified.** Ambiguity is now acknowledged, but handling conditions are not fully represented.

## S08 — Wrong intended meaning discovered later

**Condition:** Support has already explained a biological cell. The later statement specifies a battery cell.

**Expected responsibility:** Reconsider the target/context, or explicitly request a restart using the clarified meaning.

**Trace:** The visible Stage 7 destinations are language selection, core meaning and scaffolding. None returns to Stage 1/2. Stage 4 might generate a different explanation, but changing the concept there without revisiting its interpretation would hide a different responsibility inside the wrong stage.

**Conceptual result:** The initial ambiguity subtitle cannot be counted as an explicit late correction route. A bounded exception note or restart rule is needed.

**Judgement: Partly specified.** Context work exists, but its re-entry is missing. This is not evidence of an application defect; the application was not run.

## S09 — Positive self-report with incorrect understanding

**Condition:** A constructed learner says no more help is needed but still expresses an incorrect account of the process.

**Expected boundary:** A report may justify honouring a preference to pause. It cannot establish competence.

**Trace:** Stage 6 collects reported need and Stage 7 may reduce/pause. Neither CA2 nor CA3 contains an independent assessment of the learner's explanation.

**Judgement: Not resolved** for accurate competence-based adaptation. This is an evidence/capability boundary, not a newly failed mastery requirement. A quiz need not be added to preserve the current research scope, but mastery and calibrated-fading claims must remain excluded.

## S10 — Fluent but wrong generated content

**Condition:** Assume that the core explanation contains a scientific error but is fluent and well structured.

**Expected boundary:** Determine whether the representation itself can establish correctness.

**Trace:** The content can pass from core meaning to a selected aid and then receive a positive response. No arrow or label independently verifies the account. More iteration can reproduce an error.

**Judgement: Not resolved** for content verification. The diagram is a support framework, not a guarantee of scientific reliability. Independent scientific/bilingual assessment would be a different evidence source.

## Scenario synthesis

Four cases have representable routes: initial support, another example, language help and core clarification. Four are partly specified: reduced support, a skipped help choice, initial ambiguity and late context correction. Two expose unresolved capabilities: competence verification and content verification. These counts classify constructed cases; they do not estimate success rates.

C5 is supported with qualification. V2 can represent a broader set of requested-help situations than the original single-loop illustration, while the boundary cases expose remaining decision and claim limits. The result strengthens the case for focused clarification rather than another core stage or an assertion of educational effectiveness.
