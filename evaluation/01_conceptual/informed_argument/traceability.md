# Informed Argument Evaluation — Context-Aware Adaptive STEM Scaffolding Framework

## 1. Purpose

This document performs the **Descriptive: Informed Argument** evaluation of the **Context-Aware Adaptive STEM Scaffolding Framework** for INFOSYS 720 Assignment 5.

The objective is to determine whether each important part of the conceptual artefact has a defensible reason to exist and whether the framework forms a coherent response to the research problem.

This method is different from the SLR evaluation.

- The **SLR / academic literature evaluation** asks whether framework elements are supported by prior research.
- The **informed argument evaluation** asks whether the framework is logically and traceably justified from the project's own problem, PIRQOA, theoretical foundation, conceptual artefacts, and design rationale.

The central traceability chain used throughout this document is:

```text
Research Problem
→ Issue
→ Requirement
→ Theory / Literature
→ Conceptual Responsibility
→ Framework Stage
→ Research Question
→ Evaluation Judgment
```

The evaluation is analytical rather than empirical. It does not demonstrate learner outcomes or educational effectiveness.

---

# 2. Artefact Under Evaluation

## 2.1 Selected Conceptual Artefact

> **Context-Aware Adaptive STEM Scaffolding Framework**

The framework contains seven stages:

1. **Identify STEM Terminology**
2. **Interpret Technical Context**
3. **Select Language Support**
4. **Explain STEM Concept**
5. **Provide Scaffolding**
6. **Collect Learner Response**
7. **Adapt Support**

The intended sequence is:

```text
Learner STEM Inquiry
→ Identify STEM Terminology
→ Interpret Technical Context
→ Select Language Support
→ Explain STEM Concept
→ Provide Scaffolding
→ Collect Learner Response
→ Adapt Support
→ Updated Scaffolding
```

The framework is intended to integrate terminology, language, conceptual, and adaptive support into one structured process.

# 3. Research Problem and PIRQOA Basis

## 3.1 RQ1 — Terminology and Language Support

### Problem

Burmese-speaking learners may experience difficulty understanding specialized English STEM terminology because:

- technical vocabulary may be unfamiliar;
- low-resource language support is limited;
- literal translation may not preserve disciplinary meaning;
- direct translation may produce unfamiliar or misleading technical terms.

### Issue

> **Insufficient terminology and language support**

### Requirement

The artefact should:

- identify specialized STEM terminology;
- interpret technical context;
- provide context-sensitive Burmese support;
- preserve useful English technical terminology where appropriate.

### Research Question

> **RQ1: How can specialized English STEM terminology be supported for Burmese-speaking learners?**

---

## 3.2 RQ2 — Conceptual Support

### Problem

Translation alone may provide a target-language equivalent without helping a learner understand the underlying STEM concept.

### Issue

> **Insufficient conceptual learning support**

### Requirement

The artefact should provide:

- clear conceptual explanations;
- examples;
- support beyond direct translation.

### Research Question

> **RQ2: How can LLM-based support help learners understand STEM concepts beyond translation?**

---

## 3.3 RQ3 — Structured and Adaptive Support

### Problem

Unstructured LLM interaction and static explanations may provide insufficient educational scaffolding.

### Issue

> **Insufficient structured and adaptive scaffolding**

### Requirement

The artefact should:

- provide structured learner assistance;
- collect learner response;
- adjust subsequent assistance according to learner need.

### Research Question

> **RQ3: How can LLM-based scaffolding provide structured and adaptive support for Burmese-speaking STEM learners?**

# 4. Theoretical and Conceptual Basis

## 4.1 Task–Technology Fit

Task–Technology Fit provides the primary Information Systems rationale.

The project's use of TTF is based on the principle that technological capabilities should correspond to identifiable learner tasks.

The study operationalizes TTF through:

1. **Language-support fit**
2. **Conceptual-support fit**
3. **Adaptive-interaction fit**

The framework therefore should not contain arbitrary LLM capabilities merely because they are technically possible. Each capability should correspond to a learner need established through PIRQOA.

---

## 4.2 Scaffolding Theory

Scaffolding Theory provides the complementary educational rationale.

The relevant design principles are:

- structured assistance;
- support adjusted according to learner need;
- **contingency**;
- **fading**.

For the current research, contingency means that assistance changes in response to learner interaction.

Fading means support can be reduced when less assistance is reported as needed.

Important claim boundary:

> Fading based on learner self-report does not establish that objective mastery has occurred.

---

## 4.3 Relationship to the Other Conceptual Artefacts

The three conceptual artefacts form a progression:

```text
Artefact 1 — LLM-Based STEM Scaffolding Concepts
    defines what the important concepts are

Artefact 2 — Task-Aligned LLM Scaffolding Model
    explains how the major constructs relate

Artefact 3 — Context-Aware Adaptive STEM Scaffolding Framework
    specifies how support should operate
```

The selected framework therefore should not be evaluated as an isolated diagram.

It operationalizes conceptual knowledge already established through:

- STEM Terminology Identification;
- Context-Sensitive Language Support;
- Conceptual Explanation;
- Structured Scaffolding;
- Adaptive Support;
- Learner Response → Updated Scaffolding.

# 6. Stage-by-Stage Informed Argument

## 6.1 Stage 1 — Identify STEM Terminology

### Traceability chain

```text
Problem:
Specialized English STEM terminology can create a language barrier
↓
Issue:
Insufficient terminology and language support
↓
Requirement:
Identify specialized STEM terminology
↓
Knowledge base:
Automatic Term Extraction distinguishes specialized terms from ordinary vocabulary
↓
Conceptual responsibility:
Determine which technical concept requires support
↓
Framework stage:
Identify STEM Terminology
↓
RQ:
RQ1
```

### Informed argument

The learner enters the system with a **STEM learning task**, not with a request to operate a terminology-extraction tool.

The framework therefore needs an internal conceptual responsibility for determining what specialized terminology or principal STEM concept is central to the learner's inquiry.

Without this stage, later activities would lack a defined object of support.

For example:

- language support would not know which technical term requires treatment;
- context interpretation would not know which concept should be disambiguated;
- conceptual explanation could become generic rather than targeted.

The stage is consistent with TTF because it maps a technology capability directly to a learner task: difficulty with specialized terminology.

### Distinctness from Stage 2

Stage 1 answers:

> **What specialized STEM concept or term requires support?**

Stage 2 answers:

> **What does that concept mean in this particular technical context?**

These are related but distinguishable responsibilities.

### Sequence judgment

Placing terminology identification before context interpretation is defensible as a conceptual abstraction because the process first identifies a candidate technical concept and then interprets its meaning.

However, real language understanding may sometimes involve reciprocal refinement between term recognition and context.

This does not require a new framework stage.

A defensible interpretation is:

> Stage 1 identifies the initial target term/concept, while Stage 2 can refine its interpretation where context changes the intended meaning.

### Evaluation judgment

> **Strongly justified**

Stage 1 has direct problem, requirement, knowledge-base, and RQ traceability.

### Remaining limitation

The framework does not guarantee correct identification when an inquiry contains:

- multiple important concepts;
- ambiguous terminology;
- implicit concepts not explicitly named.

This is a robustness limitation rather than evidence that Stage 1 is unnecessary.

# 7. Stage 2 — Interpret Technical Context

## 7.1 Traceability chain

```text
Problem:
A technical word may have different meanings across domains
↓
Issue:
Insufficient context-sensitive terminology/language support
↓
Requirement:
Interpret technical context before selecting support
↓
Knowledge base:
Context and instructional meaning affect technical translation/explanation
↓
Conceptual responsibility:
Determine the relevant STEM/domain meaning
↓
Framework stage:
Interpret Technical Context
↓
RQ:
RQ1 and RQ2
```

## 7.2 Informed argument

Terminology identification alone is insufficient.

Terms such as:

- cell;
- current;
- force;
- inheritance;
- network

can have different meanings depending on domain.

A system that identifies a word but fails to identify the intended technical meaning could produce a structurally correct explanation of the wrong concept.

Stage 2 therefore exists to constrain downstream support.

Its outputs logically affect:

- Stage 3 — language-support choice;
- Stage 4 — conceptual explanation;
- Stage 5 — scaffolding.

This gives Stage 2 a distinct conceptual function.

## 7.3 TTF justification

The learner's task is not merely to receive a translated word.

The task is to understand the term **as used in a STEM context**.

Context interpretation therefore contributes to:

- language-support fit;
- conceptual-support fit.

## 7.4 GenAI concern: context error can propagate

The concern is logically valid.

If Stage 2 selects an incorrect disciplinary interpretation:

```text
Wrong context
→ inappropriate language treatment
→ incorrect explanation
→ inappropriate scaffolding
```

The informed argument therefore confirms the **importance** of Stage 2 and confirms context error as an important limitation.

However, this does not imply that the framework requires an additional validation stage.

At the conceptual level, it is sufficient to state that uncertainty or ambiguity in terminology/context should permit clarification before the framework commits to downstream support.

## 7.5 Evaluation judgment

> **Strongly justified, with an ambiguity-handling clarification warranted**

# 8. Stage 3 — Select Language Support

## 8.1 Traceability chain

```text
Problem:
Direct translation may produce unfamiliar or misleading technical terminology
↓
Issue:
Insufficient terminology/language support
↓
Requirement:
Provide context-sensitive Burmese support while preserving useful English terms
↓
Knowledge base:
Scientific terminology may require selective English-term preservation and contextual explanation
↓
TTF:
Language-support fit
↓
Framework stage:
Select Language Support
↓
RQ:
RQ1
```

## 8.2 Informed argument

The framework should not assume:

```text
English term → always translate to Burmese
```

because that would reproduce the translation-only problem identified by the research.

Stage 3 has a necessary decision responsibility:

> Determine the most suitable language-support form for the identified concept and context.

Possible conceptual outcomes are:

- Burmese explanation;
- English technical-term preservation;
- bilingual presentation;
- contextual combination.

This decision is central to RQ1.

## 8.3 Distinctness from Stage 2

Stage 2 determines:

> **What does the term mean in this technical context?**

Stage 3 determines:

> **How should that meaning be linguistically represented for support?**

The stages are therefore distinct.

## 8.4 GenAI concern: selection criteria are underspecified

This concern is confirmed **in part** by informed argument.

The framework should not require an implementation algorithm, but it should identify the conceptual factors that constrain the choice.

A defensible set of factors is:

1. identified terminology;
2. interpreted technical context;
3. usefulness/familiarity of the English disciplinary term;
4. need for Burmese explanation;
5. learner-expressed language preference or difficulty where available.

This preserves conceptual control without creating a deterministic language-selection algorithm.

## 8.5 Scope judgment

There is no need to add:

- language-proficiency testing;
- full learner profiling;
- translation-quality scoring;
- separate language-learning modules.

These would expand the research beyond RQ1–RQ3.

## 8.6 Evaluation judgment

> **Strongly justified, but conceptual selection criteria should be stated more explicitly**

# 9. Stage 4 — Explain STEM Concept

## 9.1 Traceability chain

```text
Problem:
Translation alone may provide terminology without conceptual understanding
↓
Issue:
Insufficient conceptual learning support
↓
Requirement:
Provide explanation beyond direct translation
↓
Knowledge base:
Native-language and multilingual educational support can help learners access technical concepts
↓
TTF:
Conceptual-support fit
↓
Framework stage:
Explain STEM Concept
↓
RQ:
RQ2
```

## 9.2 Informed argument

This stage is central to the research gap.

If the system only:

- identifies a term;
- interprets context;
- translates or preserves the term;

then the system remains primarily a terminology/translation tool.

RQ2 explicitly requires support **beyond translation**.

Stage 4 therefore has the responsibility to establish the underlying STEM meaning to be communicated.

Examples include explaining:

- what photosynthesis is;
- why electric current occurs;
- what inheritance means in object-oriented programming;
- what pH represents.

## 9.3 Distinct conceptual responsibility

A useful definition is:

> **Stage 4 determines and communicates the core STEM meaning that the learner needs to understand.**

This distinguishes it from Stage 5.

## 9.4 Evaluation judgment

> **Essential and strongly justified**

Removing Stage 4 would undermine RQ2.

# 10. Stage 5 — Provide Scaffolding

## 10.1 Traceability chain

```text
Problem:
A correct explanation may still be difficult to understand
↓
Issue:
Insufficient structured educational support
↓
Requirement:
Provide structured learner assistance
↓
Theory:
Scaffolding Theory
↓
Knowledge base:
Structured learning activities, examples, feedback, prompts and hints
↓
Framework stage:
Provide Scaffolding
↓
RQ:
RQ2 and RQ3
```

## 10.2 Informed argument

A conceptual explanation and educational scaffolding are related but not identical.

Stage 4 answers:

> **What STEM meaning should be explained?**

Stage 5 answers:

> **How should assistance around that meaning be structured so the learner can engage with it?**

Scaffolding may include:

- simplified explanation;
- example;
- analogy;
- reflective prompt;
- hint;
- change in explanatory depth.

The conceptual responsibility is therefore **organization and support**, not merely generation of more content.

## 10.3 Testing the GenAI Stage 4 / Stage 5 overlap concern

The GenAI correctly identified overlap in the existing wording because Stage 5 included:

- Simple Explanation;
- Technical Explanation.

Those can appear to duplicate Stage 4.

The informed argument therefore **confirms a wording/boundary problem**.

However, it does **not** support merging the stages.

A stronger distinction is:

### Stage 4 — Explain STEM Concept

Establish the correct conceptual meaning and core explanation.

### Stage 5 — Provide Scaffolding

Structure learner access to that meaning using appropriate support forms.

Therefore:

> **The concern is valid as a definition problem, not as evidence of conceptual redundancy.**

## 10.4 Are all scaffolding components mandatory?

No theoretical or PIRQOA requirement says every interaction must contain every support form.

A more defensible conceptual interpretation is:

> The listed components are **available scaffolding forms**, selected according to learner need and context.

This also aligns better with contingency.

## 10.5 Evaluation judgment

> **Strongly justified, but Stage 4/Stage 5 responsibilities should be clarified**

# 11. Stage 6 — Collect Learner Response

## 11.1 Traceability chain

```text
Problem:
Static one-way assistance cannot respond to learner need
↓
Issue:
Insufficient adaptive scaffolding
↓
Requirement:
Collect learner response
↓
Conceptual Artefact 2:
Learner Response guides LLM-Based Scaffolding
↓
Scaffolding Theory:
Contingency requires information about learner need
↓
Framework stage:
Collect Learner Response
↓
RQ:
RQ3
```

## 11.2 Informed argument

Adaptation requires an input signal.

Without Stage 6, Stage 7 would have no explicit learner-derived basis for adjusting assistance.

Stage 6 therefore performs a distinct responsibility:

> Obtain a learner signal indicating whether current assistance appears sufficient or whether more support is requested.

The current PoC uses:

- High;
- Medium;
- Needs Support.

The conceptual framework itself should remain broader than those exact UI labels.

## 11.3 Self-report limitation

The GenAI repeatedly identified this as the most important limitation.

The informed argument confirms the **claim boundary**.

Stage 6 provides:

> **learner-reported understanding / learner-reported support need**

It does not provide:

- measured understanding;
- objective competence;
- mastery;
- learning achievement.

This distinction is logically necessary because the framework contains no objective assessment mechanism.

## 11.4 Does this limitation invalidate Stage 6?

No.

The PIRQOA requirement is:

> collect learner response and adapt subsequent support.

It does not require objective measurement of learning.

The stage therefore satisfies the stated requirement.

The limitation affects **what can be claimed about the signal**, not whether the stage is conceptually justified.

## 11.5 Scope judgment

Adding:

- formal testing;
- quiz engines;
- knowledge tracing;
- objective mastery estimation

would materially change the research scope.

Therefore, the appropriate response is **claim discipline**, not feature expansion.

## 11.6 Evaluation judgment

> **Strongly justified for learner-responsive scaffolding, with an explicit self-report limitation required**

# 12. Stage 7 — Adapt Support

## 12.1 Traceability chain

```text
Problem:
Static support does not respond to changing learner need
↓
Issue:
Insufficient adaptive scaffolding
↓
Requirement:
Adjust subsequent assistance according to learner response
↓
Theory:
Scaffolding contingency and fading
↓
Conceptual Artefact 2:
Learner Response → Updated Scaffolding
↓
Framework stage:
Adapt Support
↓
RQ:
RQ3
```

## 12.2 Informed argument

Stage 7 is necessary because:

- collecting learner response without acting on it would not create adaptation;
- RQ3 explicitly concerns structured and adaptive support;
- Scaffolding Theory requires adjustment rather than fixed assistance.

Possible conceptual directions include:

- simplify;
- clarify;
- change example;
- alter explanatory depth;
- increase support;
- reduce support;
- conclude support.

## 12.3 GenAI concern: learner-response evaluation is not explicitly located

This concern is valid.

The framework identifies:

> **learner-response evaluation**

as a principal decision point, but the seven-stage sequence moves directly from:

```text
Collect Learner Response
→ Adapt Support
```

The most economical clarification is **not** to add an eighth stage.

Instead define Stage 7 as two conceptual responsibilities:

```text
Stage 7 — Adapt Support
1. Interpret the learner-reported support signal
2. Select an appropriate adaptive direction
```

This preserves the seven-stage structure while making the decision responsibility explicit.

## 12.4 GenAI concern: exact adaptation decision logic is underspecified

This concern is also valid, but the appropriate level of detail matters.

The conceptual framework should constrain adaptation through principles rather than an implementation algorithm.

A defensible conceptual rule is:

> Adaptation should respond to the learner-reported support need and the form of support already provided, selecting a bounded change that remains aligned with the active STEM concept.

This does not claim that a specific response always maps to one universally correct action.

## 12.5 Fading

Fading is conceptually supported when assistance is:

- reduced;
- concluded;
- made less directive.

However:

> fading after a positive self-report means support is reduced because the learner reports less need, not because objective mastery has been established.

## 12.6 Evaluation judgment

> **Essential and theoretically justified, with decision responsibility and claim boundaries requiring clarification**

# 13. Feedback Loop Evaluation

## 13.1 Current relationship

```text
Learner Response
→ Adapt Support
→ Updated Scaffolding
```

## 13.2 Why a feedback loop is necessary

Without a feedback loop, the process would be:

```text
Inquiry → response → finish
```

That would not operationalize adaptive scaffolding.

The feedback relationship therefore directly supports:

- RQ3;
- contingency;
- Adaptive Support;
- Learner Response → Updated Scaffolding.

## 13.3 GenAI concern: where does adaptation return?

The concern is conceptually important.

A learner may require:

### Different scaffold form

Return primarily to Stage 5.

### Different conceptual explanation

Reconsider Stage 4 and then Stage 5.

### Different Burmese/English presentation

Reconsider Stage 3 and downstream support.

Therefore, defining all adaptation as only:

```text
Stage 7 → Stage 5
```

may be too narrow.

## 13.4 Informed-argument resolution

A defensible bounded relationship is:

> **Adapt Support normally updates Stage 5 scaffolding, but may trigger reconsideration of Stage 3 language support or Stage 4 conceptual explanation when the learner-reported difficulty indicates that the problem concerns presentation or explanation rather than amount of support.**

This preserves:

- conceptual scope;
- structured interaction;
- bounded LLM behaviour.

It does not turn the system into unrestricted chat.

## 13.5 Should Stage 7 return to Stages 1–2?

Normally, no.

Terminology and context should remain stable within the active concept.

However, where the original interpretation is ambiguous or a learner clarification reveals a different intended concept, Stages 1–2 may need refinement.

This should be treated as an **exception for ambiguity correction**, not the normal adaptation path.

## 13.6 Evaluation judgment

> **Feedback is strongly justified; its re-entry boundary should be clarified**

# 14. Evaluation of the Major GenAI Concerns

## 14.1 Concern: Stage 4 and Stage 5 Are Redundant

### Informed-argument result

> **Not confirmed as redundancy; confirmed as a wording/boundary issue**

Reason:

- Stage 4 addresses **core conceptual meaning**;
- Stage 5 addresses **structured access/support around that meaning**.

Both are required by different aspects of PIRQOA.

### Decision

Retain both stages but clarify their definitions.

---

## 14.2 Concern: Learner Self-Report Is Too Weak

### Informed-argument result

> **Confirmed as a claim limitation, not a structural failure**

Reason:

- self-report is sufficient for the requirement to collect learner response;
- it is insufficient to demonstrate learning or mastery.

### Decision

Retain Stage 6.

Explicitly describe the input as:

> **learner-reported support signal**

Do not claim objective understanding.

---

## 14.3 Concern: Language-Support Selection Is Underspecified

### Informed-argument result

> **Partially confirmed**

The stage is necessary and defensible, but the framework should state the main conceptual factors that constrain the decision.

### Decision

Clarify Stage 3 using:

- terminology;
- technical context;
- usefulness of the English term;
- Burmese support need;
- expressed learner preference/need where available.

Do not add a deterministic algorithm.

---

## 14.4 Concern: Adaptation Decision Logic Is Underspecified

### Informed-argument result

> **Confirmed at the conceptual level**

Stage 7 should explicitly include:

1. interpretation of the learner-reported signal;
2. selection of a bounded adaptive action.

### Decision

Clarify Stage 7 rather than add a new stage.

---

## 14.5 Concern: Adaptation Loop Should Revisit Earlier Stages

### Informed-argument result

> **Partially confirmed**

Different learner-reported difficulties may require different forms of revision.

### Decision

Allow bounded reconsideration of:

- Stage 3;
- Stage 4;
- Stage 5.

Keep Stages 1–2 normally stable unless ambiguity correction is necessary.

---

## 14.6 Concern: Terminology and Context Should Be Fully Iterative

### Informed-argument result

> **Not necessary as a permanent loop**

The current order is defensible as a conceptual process.

However, context may refine the interpretation of the initially identified term.

### Decision

Retain:

```text
Stage 1 → Stage 2
```

with an ambiguity/refinement allowance rather than adding a normal reverse loop.

---

## 14.7 Concern: Generated Explanations May Be Inaccurate

### Informed-argument result

> **Confirmed as a limitation**

The framework organizes support but does not itself prove factual or pedagogical correctness.

### Decision

Do not add a separate verification stage solely from this concern because that would materially expand the conceptual artefact.

Instead state:

- generated explanation must remain aligned with interpreted STEM context;
- generation does not constitute evidence of correctness;
- system evaluation should test output behaviour separately.

---

## 17.2 C2 — Logical Coherence

### Judgment

> **Strong with clarifications**

The overall sequence is defensible:

```text
identify
→ contextualize
→ choose language support
→ establish conceptual meaning
→ structure scaffolding
→ obtain learner signal
→ adapt
```

The main clarifications are:

- Stage 1/2 may allow refinement under ambiguity;

---

## 17.3 C3 — Theoretical Consistency

### Judgment

> **Strong, provided claims remain bounded**

TTF maps to:

- language-support fit — Stages 1–3;
- conceptual-support fit — Stages 2, 4, 5;
- adaptive-interaction fit — Stages 5–7.

Scaffolding Theory maps to:

- structured assistance — Stage 5;
- contingency — Stages 6–7;
- fading — Stage 7.

The main limitation is interpretive:

> theory-informed design is not evidence that actual fit, mastery, or learning has been empirically demonstrated.

---

## 17.4 C4 — Completeness and Boundary Clarity

### Judgment

> **Mostly complete; clarification warranted**

No additional major stage is required to answer RQ1–RQ3.

The main weaknesses are:

- Stage 4/5 wording;
- Stage 3 decision criteria;
- Stage 7 response interpretation;
- feedback-loop destination.

These are best treated as refinement of existing stages rather than scope expansion.

---

## 17.5 C5 — Scenario Applicability

### Judgment

> **Not finalized in this method**

The informed argument establishes that the framework contains responsibilities that *should* be capable of supporting a realistic scenario.

However, C5 should be evaluated directly in the separate **Descriptive Scenario Evaluation**.

The informed argument therefore does not substitute for that method.

# 20. Overall Informed-Argument Judgment

The informed argument finds that the **Context-Aware Adaptive STEM Scaffolding Framework is logically defensible and strongly traceable to the research problem, PIRQOA requirements, theoretical foundation, and earlier conceptual artefacts**.

All seven stages have a clear reason to exist.

The evaluation does **not** identify a missing core function that requires an eighth stage or major redesign.

The strongest refinements are conceptual clarifications:

1. distinguish **Explain STEM Concept** from **Provide Scaffolding** more clearly;
2. state the conceptual factors constraining **Select Language Support**;
3. explicitly define Stage 6 as collecting a **learner-reported support signal**;
4. define Stage 7 as interpreting that signal before selecting an adaptive action;
5. clarify that adaptation can make a bounded return to Stage 3, 4, or 5 when appropriate;
6. allow ambiguity correction for Stages 1–2 without making the normal process unnecessarily circular;
7. state clearly that generated explanations and self-report signals do not establish educational effectiveness.

### Informed-argument overall result

> **The seven-stage structure is justified and should be retained. Clarification of stage responsibilities, decision boundaries, and claim limits is warranted, but major structural redesign is not justified by the informed argument.**

This conclusion applies only to the **Descriptive: Informed Argument** evaluation method.

It should not yet be treated as the final conceptual artefact conclusion.
