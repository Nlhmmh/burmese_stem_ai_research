# 3. Evaluation Criteria

The GenAI interview evaluated the conceptual artefact using five fixed criteria.

## C1 — PIRQOA Coverage

Whether the framework addresses:

- RQ1: terminology and language support;
- RQ2: conceptual explanation beyond translation;
- RQ3: structured and adaptive scaffolding.

---

## C2 — Logical Coherence

Whether:

- the seven stages form a coherent sequence;
- dependencies between stages are reasonable;
- the feedback loop is logically interpretable;
- stage boundaries and re-entry points are sufficiently clear.

---

## C3 — Theoretical Consistency

Whether the framework is consistent with:

### Task–Technology Fit

- language-support fit;
- conceptual-support fit;
- adaptive-interaction fit.

### Scaffolding Theory

- structured assistance;
- contingency;
- fading.

---

## C4 — Completeness and Boundary Clarity

Whether:

- important conceptual responsibilities are missing;
- stages overlap unnecessarily;
- decision points are underspecified;
- the framework remains within its intended scope.

---

## C5 — Scenario Applicability

Whether the framework can guide a realistic STEM-learning interaction from learner inquiry through adaptive support.

The interview used the scenario:

> **What is photosynthesis, and how do plants make food?**

# 6. Criterion-Level Summary

## 6.1 C1 — PIRQOA Coverage

### GenAI Judgment

> **Fully covered**

### Main Strength

All three research questions and requirements have identifiable corresponding framework stages.

### Main Weakness

Functional coverage does not demonstrate that the support produces actual learner understanding or learning.

### Confidence

> **High**

### Summary

The GenAI found strong traceability from RQ1–RQ3 to the seven framework stages.

RQ1 was associated primarily with:

- Identify STEM Terminology;
- Interpret Technical Context;
- Select Language Support;
- Explain STEM Concept.

RQ2 was associated primarily with:

- Interpret Technical Context;
- Explain STEM Concept;
- Provide Scaffolding;
- Adapt Support.

RQ3 was associated primarily with:

- Provide Scaffolding;
- Collect Learner Response;
- Adapt Support;
- the feedback loop.

No framework stage was considered clearly orphaned from the three requirements.

The major qualification was that **coverage of a required capability is not evidence that the capability is educationally effective**.

---

## 6.2 C2 — Logical Coherence

### GenAI Judgment

> **Acceptable with minor issues**

### Main Strength

The framework follows a coherent progression from inquiry interpretation through support delivery to feedback-driven adaptation.

### Main Weakness

Some relationships are insufficiently explicit, particularly:

- Stage 4 versus Stage 5;
- where the adaptation loop returns;
- whether terminology and context interpretation may need iterative interaction.

### Confidence

> **High**

### Summary

The GenAI found no fundamental contradiction in the main sequence.

The principal concerns were:

1. terminology identification may sometimes depend on context interpretation;
2. Stage 4 and Stage 5 have conceptual overlap;
3. language-support selection may need to be revisited after learner feedback;
4. Stage 7 does not clearly specify whether it can return to Stage 3, Stage 4, or only Stage 5.

These were interpreted as precision and boundary issues rather than evidence that the overall sequence is invalid.

---

## 6.3 C3 — Theoretical Consistency

### GenAI Judgment

> **Acceptable with minor issues**

### Main Strength

The framework contains identifiable counterparts for:

- language-support fit;
- conceptual-support fit;
- adaptive-interaction fit;
- structured assistance;
- contingency;
- fading.

### Main Weakness

Theoretical alignment could be overstated if:

- intended fit is described as demonstrated fit;
- learner self-report is treated as objective competence;
- fading is interpreted as proof of mastery;
- structured output is treated as evidence of educational effectiveness.

### Confidence

> **High**

### Summary

The GenAI found the theoretical mapping coherent.

However, it repeatedly distinguished between:

> **theory-informed design**

and:

> **empirically demonstrated fit or learning**

The framework can reasonably state that it operationalizes TTF and Scaffolding Theory.

It cannot infer from its structure alone that:

- task–technology fit has been empirically demonstrated;
- learners have objectively understood the concept;
- fading occurred at an educationally optimal point;
- generated explanations are pedagogically effective.

---

## 6.4 C4 — Completeness and Boundary Clarity

### GenAI Judgment

> **Mostly complete**

### Main Strength

The seven stages contain the principal conceptual responsibilities required by RQ1–RQ3 without expanding into a full LMS or broader educational platform.

### Main Weakness

The main weaknesses concern:

- Stage 4 / Stage 5 overlap;
- unclear location of learner-response evaluation;
- underspecified decision logic;
- language-selection criteria;
- feedback-loop routing.

### Confidence

> **High**

### Summary

The GenAI did **not** identify a clearly necessary missing eighth stage.

Instead, it found that several existing responsibilities require clearer definition.

The framework was also judged appropriately bounded because it does not require:

- a full LMS;
- teacher dashboards;
- quiz engines;
- social-learning functionality;
- unrestricted chatbot interaction.

## 6.5 C5 — Scenario Applicability

### GenAI Judgment

> **Applicable with minor issues**

### Main Strength

All seven stages could be instantiated using the Photosynthesis scenario.

### Main Weakness

A broad self-report such as:

> **"I partially understand"**

does not identify which part of the explanation or support requires modification.

### Confidence

> **High**

### Summary

The scenario successfully instantiated:

1. terminology identification;
2. technical-context interpretation;
3. language-support selection;
4. conceptual explanation;
5. scaffolding;

The main problem was not whether adaptation could occur, but **how the framework should choose the next adaptation**.

The scenario therefore supported applicability while exposing uncertainty in:

- language-selection decisions;
- diagnostic precision;
- adaptation choice;
- feedback-loop destination.

## T4 — Self-Reported Understanding Limits Adaptation Claims

### Related Findings

G05, G13, G24, G29, G30, G33, G46

### Summary

This was the most consistent limitation across the interview.

The GenAI repeatedly found that learner self-report can support:

- learner-responsive adaptation;
- contingency;
- fading in response to reported need.

However, it cannot establish:

- actual conceptual understanding;
- mastery;
- competence;
- objectively correct learner need;
- educational effectiveness.

### Preliminary Interpretation

This limitation should remain explicit throughout Assignment 5.

Use wording such as:

> **learner-reported understanding**

rather than:

> **measured understanding**

or:

> **demonstrated learning**

---

## T5 — Context and Generated-Content Reliability Remain Conceptual Risks

### Related Findings

G14, G27, G28, G38

### Summary

The GenAI identified two important cascading risks:

1. incorrect technical-context interpretation;
2. inaccurate or pedagogically unsuitable generated STEM explanations.

If Stage 2 interprets the concept incorrectly, later stages may still operate in a structurally correct way but provide support for the wrong meaning.

Likewise, a structured explanation is not automatically a correct explanation.

### Preliminary Interpretation

These are important conceptual limitations.

They do not necessarily require additional framework stages, but they should inform:

- limitations;
- robustness discussion;
- system testing;
- future evaluation.

# 16. Researcher Interpretation Rules for Triangulation

When completing the final interpretation, use the following approach.

## 16.1 Converging Evidence

If multiple methods support the same point:

> **Supported across methods**

Example:

- GenAI supports contingency;
- literature supports contingency;
- informed argument supports the relationship;
- scenario demonstrates it.

---

## 16.2 Partial Support

If evidence supports the general idea but not all details:

> **Partially supported**

---

## 16.3 Mixed Evidence

If methods disagree:

> **Mixed evidence**

Explain why.

---

## 16.4 GenAI-Only Concern

If a concern appears only in the GenAI interview:

> **GenAI-only concern**

Do not automatically revise the framework.

---

## 16.5 Unsupported GenAI Suggestion

If literature, informed argument, and scenario do not support the proposed change:

> **Not supported by triangulation**

# 19. Limitations of the GenAI Interview Method

The GenAI interview itself has limitations.

1. The evaluator is a language model rather than a human domain expert.
2. The response depends on the supplied prompt and conceptual framing.
3. The evaluator did not independently inspect external literature.
4. The model may identify plausible conceptual concerns that are not supported by the project's actual literature.
5. High confidence reported by the GenAI is not equivalent to empirical certainty.
6. The interview provides analytical critique, not learner or expert evidence.
7. The scenario evaluation inside the GenAI interview is not a substitute for the separate descriptive scenario method required by Assignment 5.

Therefore, all findings must be triangulated.
