## 1. Purpose

This document performs the **Conceptual Artefact Triangulation** for the **Context-Aware Adaptive STEM Scaffolding Framework** in INFOSYS 720 Assignment 5.

Four required conceptual evaluation methods have now been completed:

1. **Observational — GenAI Interview**
2. **SLR — Academic Literature**
3. **Descriptive — Informed Argument**
4. **Descriptive — Scenario Evaluation**

A Human Expert Interview is not included because it is optional and was not conducted.

The purpose of triangulation is to compare the four evidence sources rather than treat any single method as authoritative.

The triangulation asks:

- Where do the methods agree?
- Where do they only partially agree?
- Which concerns are supported by multiple methods?
- Which GenAI concerns are not confirmed by the other evidence?
- Which conceptual refinements are justified?
- Does the seven-stage framework remain defensible?
- What can and cannot be concluded from the completed conceptual evaluation?

The final researcher interpretation in this document is based on **convergence across methods**, not on the GenAI interview alone.

# 2. Artefact Under Evaluation

The evaluated conceptual artefact is:

> **Context-Aware Adaptive STEM Scaffolding Framework**

The framework contains seven stages:

1. **Identify STEM Terminology**
2. **Interpret Technical Context**
3. **Select Language Support**
4. **Explain STEM Concept**
5. **Provide Scaffolding**
6. **Collect Learner Response**
7. **Adapt Support**

The intended process is:

```text
Learner STEM Inquiry
→ Identify STEM Terminology
→ Interpret Technical Context
→ Select Language Support
→ Explain STEM Concept
→ Provide Scaffolding
→ Collect Learner Response
→ Adapt Support
→ Updated Scaffolding
```

The framework addresses three research questions:

- **RQ1:** How can specialized English STEM terminology be supported for Burmese-speaking learners?
- **RQ2:** How can LLM-based support help learners understand STEM concepts beyond translation?
- **RQ3:** How can LLM-based scaffolding provide structured and adaptive support for Burmese-speaking STEM learners?

# 3. Evaluation Methods Used

## 3.1 GenAI Interview

A structured GenAI interview evaluated:

- PIRQOA coverage;
- logical coherence;
- theoretical consistency;
- completeness and boundary clarity;
- scenario applicability.

The GenAI identified strong RQ traceability and a sound conceptual core, but raised concerns about:

- Stage 4 / Stage 5 overlap;
- language-support decision criteria;
- learner self-report;
- adaptation decision logic;
- feedback-loop routing.

The GenAI's final overall label was:

> **Requires important revision**

but it also stated that the seven-stage structure should be retained and did not require major redesign.

## 3.2 SLR / Academic Literature

The Assignment 2 literature was reused as the main academic evidence base.

The literature strongly supported:

- terminology identification;
- technical-context interpretation;
- selective/context-sensitive multilingual terminology support;
- conceptual explanation beyond translation;
- structured learning support;
- adaptive/personalized support;
- integrated educational-system design.

The literature did not directly validate:

- the exact seven-stage sequence;
- the exact language-selection rule;
- the current self-report mechanism as a measure of learning;
- the exact response-to-adaptation mapping;
- the exact feedback-loop topology.

The informed argument tested the traceability chain:

It found that:

## 3.4 Scenario Evaluation

The independent Photosynthesis scenario evaluated the actual conceptual flow using:

- bilingual terminology support;
- Simple Explanation;
- Real-World Example;
- Technical Explanation;
- Reflective Prompt;
- Hint;

It also exposed limitations around:

- unclear adaptation-selection logic;
- fading not being observed.

# 4. Triangulation Decision Rules

The following interpretations are used.

## Supported Across Methods

Three or four methods independently support the same conclusion.

## Strongly Supported

Evidence converges strongly and no method provides a substantive contradiction.

## Partially Supported

The general claim is supported, but some detail remains uncertain or method-dependent.

## Mixed Evidence

Methods provide materially different interpretations.

## Unresolved

The current evidence cannot determine the issue.

## Not Supported

A proposed concern or change is not supported when compared across the other methods.

# 7. Areas Requiring Refinement

## 7.1 Stage 4 — Stage 5 Boundary

### Evidence

- GenAI: repeated overlap concern.
- SLR: Conceptual Explanation and Structured Learning Support are distinct sub-themes.
- Informed argument: both have separate PIRQOA responsibilities.
- Scenario: both are visible, but explanatory content is included in the scaffold structure.

### Final interpretation

The evidence does not support merging the stages.

Instead:

### Revised conceptual responsibility for Stage 4

> **Establish and communicate the underlying STEM meaning that the learner needs to understand.**

### Revised conceptual responsibility for Stage 5

> **Structure and support access to that meaning using learner-facing scaffold forms such as examples, analogies, reflection, hints, and changes in explanatory depth.**

### Decision

> **Accept clarification; reject stage removal or merging.**

---

# 8. Stage 3 Language-Support Decision

### Evidence

- GenAI: language-support outcomes are clearer than decision conditions.
- SLR: context, terminology, language/task characteristics and English-term usefulness matter.
- Informed argument: the decision is necessary and should remain conceptual rather than algorithmic.
- Scenario: bilingual output works, but the actual reasoning behind term retention is not visible.

### Final interpretation

The framework should clarify that language-support selection is guided by:

1. identified STEM terminology;
2. interpreted technical context;
3. usefulness/familiarity of the English disciplinary term;
4. need for Burmese explanation;
5. learner-expressed language preference or difficulty where available.

### Decision

> **Accept conceptual clarification. Do not add a deterministic language-selection algorithm.**

---

# 9. Stage 6 Self-Report Boundary

### Evidence

- GenAI: self-report is the most important limitation.
- SLR: does not directly validate the self-report mechanism.
- Informed argument: self-report satisfies the learner-response requirement but cannot establish learning.
- Scenario: Understanding Check visibly collects perceived understanding only.

### Final interpretation

Stage 6 should be described as collecting:

> **a learner-reported support signal**

rather than measuring:

> **objective understanding**

### Decision

> **Accept explicit claim limitation.**

The framework may state:

```text
Learner-reported support signal
→ guides adaptation
```

It should not state:

```text
Measured learner understanding
→ proves learning
```

---

# 10. Stage 7 Decision Responsibility

### Evidence

- GenAI: response evaluation is not clearly located.
- SLR: adaptive support is supported, but the exact mapping is not prescribed.
- Informed argument: no eighth stage is needed; response interpretation can be included in Stage 7.
- Scenario: adaptation occurs, but the internal selection process is not visible.

### Final interpretation

Stage 7 should be conceptually clarified as:

```text
Adapt Support
1. Interpret learner-reported support need
2. Select a bounded adaptive direction
3. Update subsequent support
```

Possible adaptive directions may include:

- simplify;
- clarify;
- use another example;
- alter explanatory depth;
- modify language presentation;
- reduce support;
- conclude support.

### Decision

> **Accept clarification inside Stage 7. Do not add a new eighth stage.**

# 11. Feedback-Loop Routing

## 11.1 Normal path

The strongest evidence supports:

```text
Stage 7
→ Stage 5
```

because the Photosynthesis scenario directly demonstrates adaptation through a new example.

## 11.2 Bounded earlier-stage reconsideration

When learner-reported difficulty concerns:

### Language presentation

Stage 7 may reconsider:

> **Stage 3**

### Conceptual explanation

Stage 7 may reconsider:

> **Stage 4**

### Scaffold form

Stage 7 normally updates:

> **Stage 5**

### Ambiguous concept/context

Only exceptionally should the process revisit:

> **Stages 1–2**

### Decision

> **Accept bounded re-entry to Stages 3–5; allow Stages 1–2 only for ambiguity correction.**

This preserves an adaptive process without converting the artefact into unrestricted dialogue.

# 12. Generated-Content Reliability

### Evidence

- GenAI: high-risk concern.
- SLR: LLM performance varies across language, task and specialized context; outputs require evaluation.
- Informed argument: the framework organizes generation but does not prove correctness.
- Scenario: no independent content-validity test was performed within the conceptual scenario evaluation.

### Final interpretation

The conceptual artefact can define:

- how support is structured;
- when support changes;
- what type of support is intended.

It cannot establish:

- factual accuracy of every generated explanation;
- pedagogical appropriateness of every analogy;
- educational effectiveness.

### Decision

> **Retain as an explicit limitation. Do not add a separate verification stage solely on this basis.**

Technical/output correctness should be evaluated in the design artefact evaluation.

---

# 13. Fading

### Evidence

- GenAI: theoretically supported, but self-report cannot prove mastery.
- SLR: indirect support for adaptive assistance.
- Informed argument: fading is theoretically appropriate when less support is reported as needed.
- Scenario: `I understand` exists as an option, but the resulting fading behaviour was not observed.

### Final interpretation

Fading is conceptually justified but was **not demonstrated by the supplied scenario evidence**.

### Decision

The final paper may say:

> The framework incorporates fading conceptually by allowing support to be reduced or concluded when the learner reports less need.

It should not say:

> The evaluation demonstrated successful fading or mastery.

# 15. Refined Framework Interpretation

The evidence supports retaining the seven-stage architecture:

```text
Learner STEM Inquiry
↓
1. Identify STEM Terminology
↓
2. Interpret Technical Context
↓
3. Select Language Support
↓
4. Explain STEM Concept
↓
5. Provide Scaffolding
↓
6. Collect Learner Response
↓
7. Adapt Support
↓
Updated Support
```

with the following clarified meanings.

## Stage 1 — Identify STEM Terminology

Identify the principal specialized STEM concept or terminology requiring support.

Where terminology is ambiguous, Stage 2 may refine the interpretation.

---

## Stage 2 — Interpret Technical Context

Determine the STEM/domain meaning of the identified concept and clarify ambiguity where necessary.

---

## Stage 3 — Select Language Support

Choose a context-sensitive Burmese/English support strategy based on:

- terminology;
- technical context;
- usefulness of English disciplinary terminology;
- need for Burmese explanation;
- learner-expressed language need where available.

---

## Stage 4 — Explain STEM Concept

Establish and communicate the core STEM meaning beyond direct translation.

---

## Stage 5 — Provide Scaffolding

Structure learner support around the concept using selectable forms such as:

- examples;
- analogies;
- reflection;
- hints;
- changes in explanatory depth.

These are not necessarily mandatory in every interaction.

---

## Stage 6 — Collect Learner Response

Collect a:

> **learner-reported support signal**

indicating perceived understanding or need for additional assistance.

This is not an objective measure of learning.

---

## Stage 7 — Adapt Support

1. Interpret the learner-reported support signal.
2. Select an appropriate bounded adaptive direction.
3. Update subsequent support.

Normal return:

> Stage 5

Bounded reconsideration when needed:

> Stage 3 or Stage 4

Stages 1–2:

> only where ambiguity or interpretation needs correction.

# 18. What the Conceptual Evaluation Demonstrates

The completed four-method conceptual evaluation supports the following conclusions:

1. The seven-stage framework is strongly traceable to the research problem and PIRQOA.
2. Each stage has a defensible conceptual responsibility.
3. Terminology/context handling and selective bilingual support are justified for RQ1.
4. Conceptual explanation and structured scaffolding are both required for RQ2.
5. Learner-response collection and adaptation provide a defensible structure for RQ3.
6. The feedback relationship provides a coherent mechanism for learner-responsive scaffolding.
7. The framework remains appropriately bounded and does not require LMS, quiz, teacher-dashboard, or unrestricted-chatbot functionality.
8. The Photosynthesis scenario demonstrates that the complete framework can be instantiated in a realistic interaction.
9. The framework benefits from clarifying several decision boundaries, but evidence does not support a major structural redesign.

---

# 19. What the Conceptual Evaluation Does Not Demonstrate

The evaluation does **not** demonstrate that:

- Burmese-speaking learners learn better using the framework;
- learner self-report accurately measures understanding;
- every generated STEM explanation is correct;
- the Burmese/English balance is optimal for every learner;
- a particular adaptation is pedagogically optimal;
- fading occurs at the correct educational moment;
- Task–Technology Fit has been empirically demonstrated;
- the framework generalizes equally to every STEM domain;
- the framework is superior to alternative conceptual designs.

These claims would require evidence beyond the current conceptual evaluation.

# 20. Final Conceptual Artefact Conclusion

The combined evidence from the **GenAI interview, SLR/academic literature, informed argument, and independent Photosynthesis scenario** supports the **Context-Aware Adaptive STEM Scaffolding Framework as a coherent and well-grounded conceptual response to the PIRQOA requirements**.

The strongest convergence concerns:

- terminology identification;
- technical-context interpretation;
- selective Burmese/English support;
- conceptual explanation beyond translation;
- structured scaffolding;
- learner-response collection;
- adaptive feedback.

The evaluation also identifies several refinements.

The framework should more clearly:

1. distinguish conceptual explanation from scaffolding;
2. state the factors constraining language-support selection;
3. describe Stage 6 as a learner-reported support signal;
4. make learner-response interpretation explicit within Stage 7;
5. define bounded feedback paths to the relevant support stages;
6. maintain explicit claim limits around self-report and generated-content correctness.

These findings support **refinement rather than redesign**.

### Final triangulated judgment

> **The seven-stage framework should be retained. It provides strong conceptual coverage of RQ1–RQ3 and is supported across the four required evaluation methods. The evaluation justifies targeted clarification of stage responsibilities and adaptive decision boundaries, but does not justify adding a new core stage or substantially redesigning the artefact.**
