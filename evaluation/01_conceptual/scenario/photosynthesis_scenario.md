## Method and scope

The original seven-stage framework was applied to a photosynthesis learning situation to examine conceptual applicability. This is a descriptive scenario, not a software execution test. Code/model versions and runtime dates are therefore not required to establish its conceptual result. The supplied definition, examples, reflective prompt, hint, follow-up interface and adapted rice-plant example are reproduced below. The source states that Burmese versions were provided, but does not supply their exact wording.

## Interpretation and limits

All seven responsibilities had meaningful roles, with a coherent support–response–additional-support flow. Stage 4 core meaning and Stage 5 assistance can be distinguished without requiring an eighth stage. The scenario does not establish that language selection or adaptation was appropriately matched to learner need, nor that learning occurred.

Native criterion names and ratings are historical and not substituted for the current protocol's literature-consistency criterion. Evidence E054 records this conceptual scenario. The [design walkthrough](../../02_design/scenario/photosynthesis_scenario.md) is a separate application evaluation and cannot retrospectively fill the conceptual scenario's missing learner-response rationale.

## Seven-stage justification

### Stage 1 — Terminology

Photosynthesis is the target term. Carbon dioxide, oxygen, chloroplast and chlorophyll appear in the supplied explanation. This shows how a target and supporting vocabulary can be represented, not the accuracy of automatic extraction.

### Stage 2 — Technical context

Plant food production fixes a biological meaning. The bean-plant and rice-plant examples remain in that context. Incorrect interpretations and an iterative terminology–context loop were not exercised.

### Stage 3 — Language support

Burmese explanation was supplied while recognisable English technical terms were retained. This illustrates bilingual access. The reasons for retaining each term, learner familiarity and the quality of the exact Burmese equivalents cannot be reconstructed from the supplied excerpt.

### Stage 4 — Core explanation

The definition, plant inputs/products and chloroplast/chlorophyll account explain a process rather than supply a translated label. The equation supplies a more technical account. These are representations of core meaning, not evidence of concept acquisition.

### Stage 5 — Scaffolding

A bean plant, reflective question and leaf-factory hint make the meaning concrete and invite reasoning. Their educational purpose differs from the definition, although explanation can also serve as a scaffold. No learner response to the reflective prompt was supplied.

### Stage 6 — Learner response

An Understanding Check and three self-report choices were shown. The exact choice that triggered the rice-plant adaptation is absent. Confidence or expressed need cannot be interpreted as tested understanding.

### Stage 7 — Adaptation

The rice-plant example changes the application context without changing photosynthesis. A repeated check completes the illustrated feedback loop. The selection rule, another adaptation, fading, cap and correction routes were not demonstrated.

### Testing the Stage 4 / Stage 5 Boundary Concern

The earlier GenAI evaluation identified possible overlap between:

- Stage 4 — Explain STEM Concept;
- Stage 5 — Provide Scaffolding.

The scenario provides useful evidence for evaluating this concern.

### What Stage 4 represents in the scenario

Stage 4 can be interpreted as the **core STEM meaning**:

> Photosynthesis is the process by which plants use light energy, water, and carbon dioxide to produce glucose and release oxygen.

### What Stage 5 represents in the scenario

Stage 5 structures access to that meaning through:

- simple explanation;
- bean-plant example;
- technical explanation;
- reflective question;
- hint.

### Scenario result

The scenario confirms that some wording overlaps.

For example:

> `Simple Explanation`

and:

> `Technical Explanation`

are themselves explanations.

However, their placement inside a structured learning session demonstrates a meaningful distinction:

> **Stage 4 concerns the STEM meaning that needs to be communicated; Stage 5 concerns how support around that meaning is structured and presented.**

### Evaluation judgment

> **Boundary clarification is warranted, but the scenario does not support merging the stages.**

This agrees with the SLR and informed-argument evaluations.
