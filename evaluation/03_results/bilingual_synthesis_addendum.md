## Updated claim boundaries

INT-08 and INT-12 gain detailed supplementary support for keeping language
quality and terminology claims qualified. INT-07 remains unchanged because
bilingual correspondence does not resolve learner intention. INT-01–INT-06
retain their tested mechanical scope. No new behavioural result, independence
claim, calibrated fade claim or optimum adaptation dose follows.

RQ1 remains Partially supported. A delivered bilingual field and a correct
language-help route do not guarantee clear terminology. RQ2 remains Partially
supported. Corresponding text can share an imprecise concept explanation.
RQ3 retains bounded routing/persistence support, while semantic success and
learner benefit remain unestablished.

All thirteen Functionality Partials and the six Usability Pass/three Partials
remain unchanged. Neither translation score nor learner outcome was measured.
No new usability, black-box or white-box case was executed.

## Dependency and disagreement

The simulation run is the source of the text. Original content assessment,
new annotations, informed argument and this synthesis reuse those texts and
sometimes the same reasoning. They are not independent replications or
votes. No independent qualified bilingual verification was performed for
the supplementary annotations.

The original pH Partial and supplementary Major label are both retained.
The first is a whole-output decision using the old rubric. The second is a
local passage annotation under the new rule. They must not be silently
combined into a new three-Fail corpus total. The original 18 Pass, 71 Partial
and two Fail remain unchanged.

Selection was purposeful and informed by prior risks. Thirty-two of 91
delivered outputs were examined. The 59 other outputs were not newly
annotated. Results cannot estimate error prevalence across the full corpus,
other learners or other model outputs. Proposed Burmese corrections are
editorial, not certified reference translations.

The MQM-informed error-analysis warrant comes from
[Freitag et al. (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1162/tacl_a_00437). The
[Hevner et al. (2004)](https://doi.org/10.2307/25148625) classification applies
to the simulation generating the outputs. This new retrospective annotation
does not become a new controlled experiment simply because a rubric was used.
